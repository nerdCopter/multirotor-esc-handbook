## Timestamped claims

- [00:00-04:00] AM32 origin story: Pete asked Stefan (BLHeli_32 dev) to license a copy of BLHeli32 for a custom hardware project; Stefan declined (BLHeli32 "only for manufacturers, not individual hobbyists"). Pete wrote AM32 from scratch instead.
- [05:30-06:40] Reason didn't extend BLHeli_S: was building a sensorless speed controller needing an SPI-based position sensor (MA701/MA704 — magnetic position sensor, claimed capable of ~60,000 RPM) — not feasible in 8-bit BLHeli_S. Also wanted to do field-oriented control (FOC), needs heavy math -> 32-bit.
- [06:38] BLHeli_S is "written completely in... obscure assembly code" — Pete says he "couldn't understand a single bit."
- [13:04-14:00] Flashing mechanism CONFIRMS current KB claim: BLHeli32 bootloader is locked, ESC is "read out protected and write protected," must unlock option bits via ST-Link before chip is writable, then upload a custom AM32 bootloader (from his GitHub repo), after which future flashing goes through normal passthrough (BLHeli Suite or AM32 configurator).
- [18:00-20:11] **Variable PWM "By RPM" mechanism, specific and quotable**: "the problem with a fixed PWM rate... once you get to commutation frequencies approaching... the PWM rate, the motor actually wants to stick to those frequencies, and you see bumps in RPM." His implementation: "mine stays at 24 kilohertz until the motor is at around 11 and a half kilohertz commutation speed, at that point I start stepping up the PWM frequency proportionally with the speed of the motor... it starts off at 24 kilohertz and ends up at 48 kilohertz." Explicitly RPM/commutation-frequency based, not throttle-based.
- [20:52-21:35] Tradeoff of higher PWM freq: "as you get higher in PWM frequency you do lose a little bit of torque in the high end... your dead time gets longer proportionally... you lose a little bit of your driving power, you lose a little bit of your braking power." Says AM32's driver architecture takes dead time out of "the braking time only," so dead time roughly doubles each time PWM frequency doubles (driving on-time held constant).
- [22:16-22:37] Contrast with BLHeli_32's (non-RPM) variable PWM at the time: "he's going to do it like I do... starts off at 24 and at full throttle is at 48 kilohertz [or] 96 kilohertz... not based on commutation frequency like mine... I think the difference is BLHeli32 is based on throttle, mine's based on the speed of the motor." (NOTE: at time of the later Bardwell video xuQeJA4EGr8, BLHeli_32 did in fact ship its own "By RPM" mode in v32.8.3 — that's a later development than this interview.)
- [22:57-24:00] "Low RPM Throttle Protect": in BLHeli_32 it's "a fixed number" RPM threshold, causing problems for people running big/low-KV motors. AM32's equivalent is based on actual RPM derived from motor-pole-count + KV setting, not a fixed number — but requires the user to correctly enter motor poles in the configurator, or it defaults to behave like BLHeli's fixed value.
- [24:50-25:12] **At the time of this interview**, Pete states: "most of my firmware is for pretty generic [hardware] and I don't think most of it can support 96 kilohertz... right now I think 48 kilohertz is really... it does pretty well for the most part." This is time-stamped/dated info — may not reflect current AM32 capability (KB and other sources say up to 128kHz now).
- [26:44-27:15] **Explicit uncertainty about flight-time-gain numbers** — direct quote: "we've talked about this at quite a bit too and we've never really been able to come up with any conclusive answers yet because a lot of us just haven't done the tests." Also, at [21:00]: "I'm talking on my ass so I don't know anything about it." This is the AM32 creator himself saying the "higher PWM freq = more flight time" claim was NOT rigorously tested as of this interview. - [28:44-29:01] AM32 firmware also supports brushed motors, induction motors, ground vehicles — not multirotor-only.
