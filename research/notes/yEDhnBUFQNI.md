## Timestamped claims

- [00:40-01:04] Bardwell recommends Bluejay over JESC firmware (paid, no refund if ESC dies) and Jazz Maverick firmware (last updated August 2020, "a little bit out of date").
- [02:36-03:12] Flashing method: esc-configurator.com, browser-based (Chrome-based browser required for WebSerial/WebUSB com-port permissions), or standalone app. CONFIRMS KB's flashing method claim.
- [04:23-04:52] New-with-Bluejay option: PWM frequency selectable (24/48/96kHz). Explains WHY it's selectable on Bluejay but not needed as a separate flash choice on BLHeli_32: "on BLHeli32 ESCs you don't have to flash different firmware to change the motor PWM frequency, you can just change it in software, but on these BLHeli-S ESCs they don't have enough space for the code" — PWM frequency is baked into the flashed firmware binary on 8-bit Bluejay, not a runtime-changeable setting. **This is new, not currently in bluejay.md, and is an important mechanical distinction (BLHeli32/AM32: runtime setting; Bluejay/8-bit: must reflash to change PWM frequency).**
- [04:45-05:44] **PWM frequency sizing guidance, direct and specific (CONTRADICTS current KB framing that treats 96kHz as generally risky/deprecated for small motors):**
  - "For very small quads like toothpicks and smaller, down to 65mm tiny whoops, generally the higher the PWM frequency the longer the flight time you're going to get... much more true for 65mm tiny whoops than for 3-inch toothpicks... maybe even at the 3-inch size it doesn't make sense anymore."
  - "Some people would argue that for larger, like freestyle quads, you'd want 96kHz. The problem is higher PWM frequencies also significantly reduce braking torque and can make the quad handle worse — for larger freestyle quads, 5 inches and up, you probably want 24kHz."
  - His stated picks: **tiny whoops -> 96kHz**, **~3" -> 48kHz** (he picks 48kHz for the demo 3" quad in the video), **5"+ freestyle -> 24kHz**.
  - No mention of desync risk from 96kHz on small motors anywhere in this video — his framing is purely flight-time vs. braking-torque/handling, not desync-proneness.
- [09:57-11:01] Motor timing on Bluejay: range is "medium-high to high." Higher timing decreases odds of desync but runs motors hotter/less efficient, "can also make a little bit more power." Racers often run highest available timing. "There isn't really a compelling reason to turn the timing down that I can think of" except possibly "really big, low-KV motors." Default timing "is probably fine."
- [11:01-11:26] Demag compensation: "safety setting" — if ESC thinks motor desynced, does "blind commutation" (commutates without zero-crossing detection). "Low is a fine value for most people. If you have a particularly desync-prone quadcopter you can set it to High and it may fix your desyncs." "Basically nobody should set it to Off." CONFIRMS current KB guidance direction.
- [11:29-11:52] Beep strength / Beacon strength — cosmetic/lost-model settings, not previously detailed in KB. Minor, low priority to add.
- [11:52-12:20] "RPM Power Protection" (Bluejay's actual name for what KB calls "rampup power"): controls how hard the ESC accelerates an *already-spinning* motor. Turning it up = more responsive but "greater chance of a desync or a fried motor." **Note: KB's bluejay.md doesn't currently name this parameter "RPM Power Protection" — worth using the correct in-firmware name.**
- [08:52-10:00] Startup Min/Max Power: Bardwell explicitly does NOT give numeric tuning guidance — direct quote: "I don't know how you tune that value, just raise it until you fry a motor, I really don't [know]... we're just going to leave that at the default." **This directly contradicts the KB's confidence in specific numeric ranges (Startup Min 1100-1200, Max 1200-1250) — the creator/major community voice in this video gives NO numbers and says he doesn't know the tuning method. Numeric ranges in bluejay.md remain unsourced by this video.**
- [12:23-12:29] **New setting not currently in KB**: "Dithering" — should basically always be on, "unless you're using 24k PWM frequency, because without it your motor resolution will be extremely low."

## KB cross-check (bluejay.md, current tracked content)
- CONTRADICTION: bluejay.md currently frames 96kHz as "Higher Desync Risk" / caution, and recommends 48kHz broadly for "1S-4S quads and brushless TinyWhoops." This video (Bardwell, the video actually cited as the source for this section) explicitly recommends **96kHz specifically for tiny whoops/toothpicks** for flight time, and only steers toward 24kHz for **5"+ freestyle** due to braking-torque loss — the opposite size mapping current text implies. The two concerns (desync-proneness vs. flight-time/braking-torque) are different axes; KB conflates them. Recommend: reframe as "if desyncing, drop PWM frequency" (troubleshooting) vs. "for flight time on tiny whoops, Bardwell recommends 96kHz" (baseline/default) — these can coexist but need to be stated as separate concerns, sourced separately.
- NEW content not in KB: PWM frequency is flash-time-fixed on 8-bit Bluejay (not a live setting like AM32/BLHeli32); "Dithering" setting; correct in-firmware name "RPM Power Protection."
- Startup Min/Max Power numeric ranges in current bluejay.md are NOT sourced to this video — Bardwell explicitly disclaims knowing tuning numbers. Numbers remain unverified community folklore (already partially flagged by another review pass per KB state).
